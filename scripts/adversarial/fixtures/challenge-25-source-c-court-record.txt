REGISTER OF THE VESTED COUNCIL SESSION TWO HUNDRED FOURTEEN

The Council's clerks kept this register in the old form, entering each petition in the order it was received and closing each entry with the finding and the date, regardless of how the petitioner or the petitioner's counsel felt about the outcome. Sessions ran through the winter months, when the harbor traffic slowed enough that the Council chamber could be spared for hearings rather than given over to customs business, and the register for this particular winter runs to eleven entries, of which the following is the fourth, copied here in full for the benefit of any later clerk who must cross-reference it against the fleet's own separate losses ledger.

In the two hundred and fourteenth year of the Council calendar, a petition was entered before the Vested Council on behalf of the petitioner recorded there under the estate style of Vessa's Kade. The petition sought reinstatement of naval rank following the inquiry into the loss of the vessel called the Ardent Reach at the shoals off Coriveil, an inquiry the register elsewhere describes at some length and need not repeat here beyond noting that it ran to nine sittings before a finding was reached. Clerk Bariun Thess recorded the proceedings that winter and noted, as he customarily did for absent petitioners, that the petitioner did not appear in person, the estate being represented instead by hired counsel from the coastal registry, a common enough arrangement for holders whose land lay some distance from the Council seat and who could not easily spare the season's travel for a single hearing, however consequential that hearing might prove to the estate's own standing before the Council in later years.

The register further states that Vessa's Kade had held the coastal holding since inheriting it from an aunt on the maternal line, some years before ever taking to naval service at all, and it is from that holding, the clerks' own index notes, that the estate itself takes its name, the same holding by which the petitioner continued to be known in every later entry this office could locate. The Vested Council's inquiry of that year found that Vessa's Kade had acted against the harbor pilot's recorded warning when the fleet was ordered into the shoals, and the finding was entered without dissent among the seated members, who deliberated for the better part of an afternoon before returning it. On that finding, naval rank was stripped in full and the coastal holding was placed under a lien pending settlement of the fleet's broader losses from that same engagement, a settlement the register notes was still unresolved three sessions later, to the evident frustration of the counsel handling the matter.

The petition for reinstatement, entered that winter by counsel acting on behalf of Vessa's Kade, was heard at length and denied without further comment from the bench, the presiding member noting only that the finding of the original inquiry stood unaltered by anything counsel had raised that session. Bariun Thess's final note in the register for that entry states that no further correspondence was received from the holding after the denial was delivered, and no further petition was ever entered on the same matter in any later session the clerk's office could locate, though the estate itself continued to appear in the annual tax rolls under its own name for some years afterward.

Later entries in the register, cross-referenced by the clerk's own index, continue to refer to the holder by the same coastal estate rather than by any given name, a habit the register's earlier volumes show was already old before this particular session was convened, and one the index attributes to a general Council preference for identifying petitioners by the land that answers for them rather than by whatever name a family happened to give a child, a preference the clerks defend on the grounds that land, unlike a name, cannot be disputed by a rival branch of the same family and so makes a steadier heading for a register meant to outlast any one petitioner's own lifetime. The lien on the coastal holding was not lifted until a subsequent Council session, some years after this entry, when the settlement of the fleet's losses was finally closed out and the register records the lien's release in a single terse line at the foot of that later session's own final page, without further remark on the petitioner or the petition that had opened the matter.
