The lighthouse keeper at Fastnet Rock kept a logbook of every ship that
passed within sight of the beam. On the night of the storm, the entry
read only: "Barometer falling. Gulls silent since noon. No vessels."

By morning the logbook had a second hand in it — not the keeper's own,
cramped and slanting the way it always was, but a wider, rounder script
that filled the rest of the page: "He went out to the gallery at
half past two and did not come back in. I have kept the light burning
since. My name is Aoife Devane and I do not know how I came to be here."

The relief boat found both of them a week later, sharing the small
kitchen table, sharing the log, sharing the watches, as if two keepers
had always tended Fastnet and the roster simply forgot to say so. No
one could explain the second name. No one asked Aoife to leave.
